NEAB: North-East Atlantic–Baltic

Five pyGETM models from the NE Atlantic at 6.5 km down to the Danish straits at 0.25 km, coupled by two-way nesting. Each level has its own grid, time step and cores; all of them compute at the same time. A coarse version at half the resolution is used for all exploratory work.

Bathymetry, as the NSe production setup

  • Raw: ocean-prep bathymetry-regrid, conservative regridding of GEBCO / EMODnet, once per level.
  • Processing: thalweg corridors, local minimum depths, basins, rx0 ≤ 0.2.
  • Fixes in a second pass, the same groups as NSe (Irish Sea, Great Belt, Little Belt, Øresund). The Little Belt is closed in the raw grids and opened by its thalweg group.

Since then the order of the exchanges in a sync has changed: one halo update per sync, the children's data sent up first (bit-identical results). The same 31-day run now takes 21 minutes on 64 cores. Timers in the halo updates showed that most of what had looked like nesting work was waiting for the other models; the nesting's own work (restriction, flux matching, ring values) is 6–14 % of the time.

Open issue

The tide east of the Skagerrak amphidrome

Over most of the North Sea NEAB's M2 is within 10–15 % of TPXO9. From the Danish North Sea coast into the Skagerrak and the Kattegat it is about twice too large (Skagen 0.29 m against 0.14 m observed). The error is the same at both resolutions, and a bottom roughness map from the seabed substrate does not remove it.

The amphidrome in front of the German Bight lies about 0.3° south-west of TPXO9's. The region off Jutland and the Skagerrak lies between that amphidrome and the Norwegian one. Its small amplitudes are very sensitive to where the amphidromes are, so a small shift doubles them there. The most likely cause is too little dissipation in the south-eastern North Sea:

  • The Wadden Sea flats are not in the bathymetry: after the regridding they are land or have no depth (the sources refer to the lowest astronomical tide).
  • The models mask water shallower than 5 m.
  • The tide coming in from the Atlantic is also 5–17 % too large (no tidal potential, 12 km cells over the north-western shelf), but that is a smaller effect.

Much more friction over the shallow water that is already there does almost nothing, except locally. So it is the missing area of shallow water and flats, and their volume, that matters, more than the friction on them. The remedy is a Wadden Sea bathymetry with its intertidal flats, and the Wadden Sea level proposed above. Before production runs, every level's bathymetry has to be checked carefully.

A Wadden Sea level

A sixth level, wadden, nests in north_sea_baltic over the Wadden Sea from Den Helder to Skallingen (4.6–9.2°E, 52.9–55.7°N) at ratio 3, about 1.5 km in the coarse setup, with the open North Sea in front of it. Its bathymetry includes the intertidal flats:

  • EMODnet without its land mask: the flats keep their height above the lowest astronomical tide (LAT).
  • Land is taken from the Natural Earth land polygons, not from the sign of the depth, so the polders behind the dikes, below sea level, stay land.
  • The depths are taken from LAT to mean sea level: MSL − LAT is estimated from TPXO9 as the sum of its constituents' amplitudes, about 1.2–1.5 m along the Dutch coast, 2–2.5 m in the German Bight and 1 m at Esbjerg.
  • Water up to 1 m above MSL is kept wet. pyGETM's drying and flooding handles the flats.

Most of the flats then lie 0–2 m below MSL: 3,800 km² that fall dry at the lowest tide are water in this level, where north_sea_baltic has land.

The flats lower the Skagerrak and off Jutland by 15–20 %, and the Kattegat gauges with them, more than any of the sensitivity runs above. But they also take 15–25 % of the tide in the German Bight and the Wadden Sea itself, which was about right or too small before. With z0 0.015 m the drag on water less than a metre deep is several times that in 20 m, so the flats likely dissipate too much. A second run with z0 0.002 m in the Wadden Sea level only (north_sea_baltic keeps 0.015 m) helps on both sides. The Skagerrak comes down further, to 1.81 times TPXO9, and Skagen to 0.22 m. In the Wadden Sea about half of the lost tide comes back, but it is still lower than in the control run, and the error over all gauges is 0.19 m against 0.17 m. Lower friction also lowering the Skagerrak shows that the flats act through where the tide goes, not only through what they dissipate.

A third run tests a depth-dependent z0 in the Wadden Sea level: z0 = 0.015 m × min(1, H/10 m), at least 0.0005 m, with H the depth below MSL (--z0-depth 10). pyGETM's drag already uses the actual water depth, so with a constant z0 the drag coefficient on a flat 1 m deep is 0.013, against 0.004 in 20 m. With z0(H) it stays at about 0.005 on the flats, while the channels and the open sea, 86 % of the level's cells, keep 0.015 m. The result is almost that of the constant 0.015 m: the friction on the flats themselves hardly matters. What the 0.002 m run changed was the friction in the channels and in the sea in front of the flats. Even with it, the Wadden Sea level lowers the German Bight by 10–15 % against the control run. That points to the flats themselves, their area and height: MSL − LAT from the amplitude sum is an upper bound, so the flats may lie too deep and take too much water. Checking them against a survey-based bathymetry with heights is the next step, and production runs need a closer look at the bathymetry in any case.

Two more runs raise the flats: MSL − LAT is taken from the M2 amplitude alone (wadden_bathymetry.py --msl-lat m2), 0.01–1.52 m instead of 0.05–3.02 m. That leaves 1 km³ less water in the part shallower than 5 m, and 140 more cells above MSL. With z0 0.002 m in the Wadden Sea level, the German Bight comes back to 0.93 of TPXO9, and Helgoland, Cuxhaven and Wilhelmshaven to within 10–20 % of the gauges. The Skagerrak, however, drops less (1.98 instead of 1.81 times TPXO9). Over all gauges the two flat heights are equal (0.18 m against 0.19 m), and both are still above the control run's 0.17 m. Higher flats help the Wadden Sea and cost the Skagerrak, so the flats alone do not settle it. Two tests of the North Sea depths are under way. In the first, all levels are taken from LAT to MSL: EMODnet, and GEBCO built from it, give depths below LAT, so the shelf is 1–3 m too shallow and the tide too slow. In the second, north_sea_baltic takes its North Sea depths from EMODnet instead of GEBCO: GEBCO 2026 is a patchwork there, about 0.5 m shallower than EMODnet in the south-eastern North Sea and 2–4 m shallower off Jutland, with straight seams between its sources.

Reference

Tidal maps for model comparisons

As a common reference for comparing models, the twelve TPXO9-atlas constituents (M2, S2, N2, K2, K1, O1, P1, Q1, M4, MS4, MN4, 2N2) are cut to the AMM7 area, extended east and north to hold the whole Baltic: 20°W–31°E, 40–66°N, on TPXO9-atlas's own 1/30° grid. The file tpxo9_atlas_amm7_baltic.nc (15 MB, in TPXO9/maps beside the TPXO9 data) holds, for every constituent:

  • the amplitude (m) and the Greenwich phase lag (°): the cotidal lines are the contours of the phase;
  • the complex amplitude (re, im), for interpolation onto a model grid without the jump at 0/360°;
  • TPXO9's depth and water mask.

These are harmonic constants without nodal corrections. Only water connected to the Atlantic is kept. Inside the box the Mediterranean is cut off (Gibraltar lies south of 40°N), and TPXO9-atlas has amplitudes of metres there that are not real, in the Adriatic. TPXO9 is fitted to altimetry and tide gauges, so it is a good reference offshore and on the open shelf. In the Wadden Sea, the inner Danish waters and the Baltic, with tides of a few centimetres, it is a guide, to be used together with gauges. In the Baltic its local patches show edges against its coarser global solution (Gulf of Finland, Gulf of Riga, 24°E in the Bothnian Bay). The file and the figures come from NEAB/scripts/tide_reference.py.

Tidal series

The same TPXO9 constituents give time series at any point, predicted as pyGETM does at its open boundaries (all twelve constituents, nodal corrections), with model runs on top (NEAB/scripts/tide_series.py). January 2020 in the Bristol Channel (Hinkley Point) and at Saint-Malo, two of the largest tides in the area, both with a range of over 11 m at springs, against NEAB's control run. The series are taken at the nearest TPXO9 node with tidal constants: at Hinkley Point that is 11 km out in the channel (M2 3.72 m against 3.92 m at the gauge), at Saint-Malo 3 km off (3.68 m against 3.67 m). NEAB's spring tides in the Bristol Channel are about 0.5 m too small, and its neaps at Saint-Malo a little too large. Part of the rms difference comes from NEAB's hourly output, which cuts the peaks.

Uses

Boundaries for other setups

A NEAB run can write hourly elevation, transports and velocities of its levels. nesting-boundaries samples them at another model's open-boundary points, taking at every point the finest NEAB level with water there. The result is a file in the layout of the OceanICU NSe setup, which pyGETM reads on its own grid without interpolation. The first user is DKm, the Danish waters with their fjords: its root and its Belt Sea level take their open boundaries from NEAB, TPXO9 or CMEMS. Through NEAB, DKm inherits the Kattegat error described above. With TPXO9 at its boundaries, DKm has the Kattegat tide right.

Code

What the nesting changes in pyGETM

The nesting itself is a new package, pygetm.nesting: about 4,000 lines of Python with mpi4py, holding the geometry of the levels, the MPI exchanges, restriction, flux matching and the time loop. It attaches to each model through a small set of hooks in pyGETM's core: about 400 changed lines in twelve files. All of them are optional. A model without nesting runs exactly as before (the optional arguments default to nothing), with one exception: a bug fix in the transport damping.

NEAB is built with the two-way nesting for pyGETM developed by Bolding & Bruggeman (fork bolding/pygetm, branch nesting-mpmd). The code of the setup is not public yet. Bathymetry: GEBCO, EMODnet; tides: TPXO9; gauges: GESLA. Related: DKm.
